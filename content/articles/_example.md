# Completing the square

$$
x^2+6x+5=(x+3)^2-4
$$

> [!NOTE] Start here
> Half the coefficient of $x$, square it, and add and subtract that value without changing the expression.

## Why the method works

A perfect-square trinomial has the form $(x+a)^2=x^2+2ax+a^2$. Matching the middle coefficient tells us which square to build.

## Worked example

> [!EXAMPLE] Rewrite in vertex form
> Start with $x^2+6x+5$.

$$
x^2+6x+5=x^2+6x+9-9+5
$$

$$
=(x+3)^2-4
$$

## Common mistakes

- Adding a number without subtracting the same number.
- Forgetting to square half of the middle coefficient.
- Losing the constant term while regrouping.

## Keep these ideas

- Completing the square rewrites an expression; it does not change its value.
- The method reveals the vertex immediately.
- Expanding the result is a fast check.
