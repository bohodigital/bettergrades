# Unit P5: Rational Functions

**Governing question.** How does division create restrictions, discontinuities, asymptotes, and separated branches of behavior?

This unit studies how polynomial quotients create missing inputs and separated graph branches.

## Unit lessons

## P5.1. Rational functions and their domains

**Lesson outcome.** Identify rational functions and determine all excluded inputs from the original denominator before simplifying.

### Opening

A rational function P/Q excludes every real input where the original denominator Q is zero.

This matters because the rest of Precalculus will repeatedly use rational functions and their domains to decide what a formula means, what a graph can show, and which conclusions remain valid.

### Before you begin

1. Factor numerator and denominator.
2. Preserve original restrictions.
3. Use sign and asymptotic notation.

### Exact learner exposition

Factor the denominator, solve Q=0, record exclusions, and partition the domain into intervals of continuity.

A rational function can have all-real domain when its denominator has no real zero.

A symbolic answer is not complete by itself. In this lesson, the same claim must also be readable through numerator-denominator role map, domain intervals, or another equivalent representation.

A complete response states what the result means. When the setting is contextual, include units and a meaningful domain. When the result is numerical, distinguish an exact value from an approximation and state the precision used.

### Common mistake

A frequent error is simplifying before recording the original exclusions.

## P5.2. Equivalent formulas, different functions, and holes

**Lesson outcome.** Identify removable discontinuities caused by common factors and distinguish formula equivalence from function equality.

This matters because the rest of Precalculus will repeatedly use equivalent formulas, different functions, and holes to decide what a formula means, what a graph can show, and which conclusions remain valid.

2. Preserve original restrictions.
3. Use sign and asymptotic notation.
